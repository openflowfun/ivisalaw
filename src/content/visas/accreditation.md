whoItsFor:
  lead: >-
    Accreditation is about your business, not the vacancy. Immigration New Zealand is deciding
    whether you are a fit employer of migrant workers at all.
  fits:
    - You want to hire a migrant worker and are not yet accredited.
    - Your accreditation is due to expire and you need to renew.
    - You place workers with a third party and need triangular accreditation.
    - You are hiring more people than your current accreditation type allows.
  doesntFit:
    - You have a specific role to fill and are already accredited — you need a Job Check.
    - You are not an employer, and the worker would be a contractor.
    - Your business is on an employer or immigration stand-down list.

eligibility:
  - title: Choosing the right accreditation type
    detail: >-
      Standard covers up to five migrant workers. High-volume covers six or more. Triangular is
      for businesses that place workers with a controlling third party — labour hire, and some
      contracting models. Applying for the wrong type means starting again, and upgrading later
      carries its own fee.
    figure:
      value: 5 workers
      label: Where standard stops and high-volume begins
      source: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/for-employers/getting-accreditation-or-approval-to-hire/employer-accreditation-for-the-aewv/applying-for-aewv-employer-accreditation-process-steps/check-if-you-can-apply-for-aewv-employer-accreditation-and-what-type/
  - title: A viable and genuinely operating business
    detail: >-
      Immigration New Zealand assesses whether the business is real and trading, and whether it
      meets the financial requirements. Bankruptcy or a No Asset Procedure rules you out, as
      does appearing on an employer stand-down list, an immigration stand-down list, or a
      permanent ban.
  - title: The right legal entity applies
    detail: >-
      Accreditation is held by the entity that holds the NZBN. Group structures catch people
      out here: the company that employs the worker is the company that needs accreditation,
      which is not always the one that runs the recruitment.
  - title: Extra conditions for triangular accreditation
    detail: >-
      Triangular applicants need at least 12 months of history placing staff in New Zealand,
      and must guarantee that at least 15% of the staff they place are New Zealand citizens or
      residents working a minimum of 30 hours a week. It is the most demanding type and the
      most expensive by a wide margin.
    figure:
      value: 12 months, 15%
      label: Triangular thresholds
      note: Placement history, and the share of placed staff who must be citizens or residents.
  - title: Obligations that continue after approval
    detail: >-
      You must comply with New Zealand employment law and minimum standards and with the
      Immigration Act 2009, meet every commitment made in the application, and tell Immigration
      New Zealand about changes to your organisation or when an AEWV worker stops working for
      you. These are ongoing conditions, not one-off declarations.
  - title: What a breach costs
    detail: >-
      Suspension or revocation of accreditation, a stand-down period, infringement notices and
      fees, prosecution, and in serious cases a permanent ban from supporting visa applications.
      Revocation affects every worker you currently sponsor, not just the matter in question.

process:
  - actor: You
    title: Confirm which type you need
    detail: >-
      Count the migrant workers you expect to hire over the accreditation period, and establish
      whether anyone will be placed with a third party. Both answers change the type and the fee.
  - actor: You
    title: Get the entity and records straight
    detail: >-
      Confirm which NZBN entity will employ the worker, and that employment agreements, wage
      and time records and holiday records meet minimum standards. This is what an audit looks
      at later.
  - actor: You
    title: Apply online
    detail: >-
      Declarations about your business, your compliance history and the commitments you are
      making. Everything declared here is what you will be held to.
    duration: Allow 2–4 weeks
  - actor: Immigration NZ
    title: Assessment
    detail: >-
      Straightforward applications move quickly. Complex ownership, a compliance history, or a
      triangular application take longer.
  - actor: You
    title: Job Check for each role
    detail: >-
      Accreditation alone hires nobody. Every role needs its own Job Check before your candidate
      can lodge anything.
    duration: Valid 6 months each
  - actor: Your candidate
    title: The visa application
    detail: >-
      Your worker applies with the job token issued from your approved Job Check. Errors at your
      end delay theirs.
  - actor: You
    title: Renew before it lapses
    detail: >-
      Initial accreditation runs 12 months. Renewal extends it to 24 months for standard and
      high-volume, 12 for triangular. Immigration New Zealand emails a reminder two weeks out.
    duration: 24 months after first renewal

whatGoesWrong:
  - problem: The wrong entity applies
    consequence: >-
      In group structures the company that recruits is often not the company that employs.
      Accreditation granted to the wrong NZBN does not cover the employment.
    fix: Establish which entity signs the employment agreement, and accredit that one.
  - problem: Underestimating the number of hires
    consequence: >-
      Standard accreditation caps at five migrant workers. Businesses that grow past it mid-term
      find their next hire blocked.
    fix: >-
      Count over the whole accreditation term, not the current vacancy. Upgrading costs $505;
      guessing low costs a hiring freeze.
  - problem: Treating the commitments as paperwork
    consequence: >-
      Everything declared at application is an ongoing obligation. Breaches lead to suspension
      or revocation, which affects every worker you sponsor rather than one file.
    fix: >-
      Read the commitments as terms you will be audited against, and make someone accountable
      for them.
  - problem: Employment records that do not meet minimum standards
    consequence: >-
      Wage and time records, holiday records and employment agreements are exactly what an audit
      examines. Gaps found there put accreditation at risk regardless of immigration compliance.
    fix: Fix the employment side before applying, not after Immigration New Zealand asks.
  - problem: Letting accreditation lapse
    consequence: >-
      An expired accreditation stops new hires and invalidates Job Checks. Renewing something
      that expired more than 12 months ago earns only a 12-month term instead of 24.
    fix: >-
      Diarise the expiry independently of the reminder email, which arrives only two weeks out.
  - problem: Assuming accreditation lets you hire
    consequence: >-
      Accreditation is about the business. Every individual role still needs its own Job Check,
      and employers who miss this lose weeks they thought they had banked.
    fix: Plan the Job Check timeline into the offer, not after it.

faq:
  - q: How long does accreditation last?
    a: >-
      Initial accreditation runs 12 months. Renewal extends it to 24 months for standard and
      high-volume, or 12 months for triangular. Renewing something that expired more than a year
      ago gets you 12 months rather than 24.
  - q: Does accreditation let me hire straight away?
    a: >-
      No. It qualifies the business. Each role then needs its own Job Check, which is where
      advertising and market rate are assessed, and only then can your candidate apply.
  - q: What if I need to hire more people than my type allows?
    a: >-
      Upgrade from standard to high-volume for $505. That is materially cheaper than a fresh
      application, but only available while your accreditation is current.
  - q: Do I need triangular accreditation?
    a: >-
      Only if you place workers with a controlling third party. It requires 12 months of
      placement history and a guarantee that at least 15% of placed staff are New Zealand
      citizens or residents on 30 or more hours a week.
  - q: What happens if we get audited?
    a: >-
      Immigration New Zealand looks at whether you have met your commitments and complied with
      employment law and the Immigration Act. Outcomes range from a warning to revocation,
      infringement fees, prosecution and a permanent ban. Talk to us before you respond.
  - q: Can I recover the costs from the worker?
    a: >-
      Accreditation and Job Check fees are the employer's cost and recovering them from a
      migrant worker is a compliance risk. If cost recovery is on the table, get advice before
      you act on it.
