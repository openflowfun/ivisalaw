whoItsFor:
  lead: >-
    Assessed on the relationship, not on what you do for a living. That makes it the most
    accessible residence route for the people it fits, and the least forgiving on evidence.
  fits:
    - Your partner is a New Zealand citizen or resident.
    - You have been living together for at least 12 months.
    - You can document the relationship across its full span.
    - Your occupation would not get you there on points or the Green List.
  doesntFit:
    - Your partner holds a work or student visa rather than residence — the temporary partner visa applies.
    - You have been together 12 months but not living together for 12 months.
    - Your partner has residence through Australian citizenship but does not live in New Zealand.

eligibility:
  - title: Your partner is a New Zealand citizen or resident
    detail: >-
      A partner on a work or student visa cannot support residence — that is the temporary
      partner visa instead. Where their residence comes through Australian citizenship or
      permanent residency, they must actually be living in New Zealand.
  - title: Twelve months living together
    detail: >-
      Not twelve months together. Twelve months living together, at the date you apply, in a
      relationship that is genuine and stable. Long-distance years before you moved in do not
      count toward it, however real the relationship was.
    figure:
      value: 12 months
      label: Living together before you can apply
      source: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/partner-of-a-new-zealander-resident-visa/
  - title: Genuine and stable, in evidence
    detail: >-
      Immigration New Zealand weighs the duration of the relationship and of living together,
      whether finances and responsibilities are shared, time spent together, and whether others
      recognise the relationship. Each is assessed on documents rather than on assertion.
  - title: What actually persuades
    detail: >-
      A tenancy or mortgage in both names for the full 12 months, joint accounts or a consistent
      pattern of transfers, bills and correspondence to the shared address, photographs spread
      across the period rather than clustered around a wedding, travel together, and statutory
      declarations from people who know you both. The shape of the record matters more than its
      volume.
  - title: The supporting partner's obligations
    detail: >-
      They must be at least 18, or 16 to 17 with parental permission, meet character
      requirements, and sponsor you. Where they have supported a previous partner for residence
      there are limits on doing so again, which is worth checking before you plan around it.
  - title: Health, character and children
    detail: >-
      Medical examinations and police certificates as for any residence application, and
      certified English translations of anything not in English. Dependent children aged 24 or
      younger can be included, with those aged 21 to 24 needing to show financial dependence.

process:
  - actor: You
    title: Establish the 12-month date
    detail: >-
      Fix the date you began living together, and what evidences it. Everything else is built
      backwards from that date, and applying before it is the most common avoidable decline.
  - actor: You
    title: Build the evidence across the whole relationship
    detail: >-
      Not the last few months. Assemble it chronologically so the file reads as a continuous
      record rather than a folder of exhibits.
    duration: Allow 3–6 weeks
  - actor: Your partner
    title: Sponsorship and character
    detail: >-
      Your supporting partner completes their part, including character disclosure and any
      history of previously supporting a partner.
  - actor: You
    title: Lodge the application
    detail: >-
      With the relationship evidence, both parties' identity documents, medicals, police
      certificates, and any children being included.
  - actor: Immigration NZ
    title: Assessment
    detail: >-
      Assessed on the relationship. Interviews and requests for further information are common
      here and are not in themselves a bad sign.
    duration: 80% within 7 months

whatGoesWrong:
  - problem: Applying before the 12 months is complete
    consequence: >-
      The requirement is 12 months living together at the date of application. Lodging at ten
      months does not get assessed early — it gets declined, and the fee is gone.
    fix: Fix the start date and diarise it. There is no benefit to lodging a day before.
  - problem: Counting time together rather than time living together
    consequence: >-
      Couples together three years but cohabiting for eight months read as eight months. The
      long-distance period is real but does not count toward the 12.
    fix: >-
      Date the cohabitation from something documentary — a tenancy, a change of address, a bill —
      not from memory.
  - problem: Everything in one partner's name
    consequence: >-
      A shared home with a single-name tenancy, single-name bills and no joint account produces
      almost no evidence of living together, which is exactly what is being tested.
    fix: >-
      Add the second name to the tenancy, a utility and an insurance policy now. The record has
      to accumulate; it cannot be assembled afterwards.
  - problem: Evidence clustered around one event
    consequence: >-
      A hundred wedding photographs and nothing else demonstrates a wedding, not a relationship
      over time. Assessors are looking for continuity.
    fix: Spread the evidence across the whole period, thinly if necessary, rather than deeply at one point.
  - problem: Assuming an interview means trouble
    consequence: >-
      Couples panic at a request for further information or an interview and start supplying
      inconsistent detail, which creates the problem they feared.
    fix: >-
      Treat it as routine, answer precisely, and get advice before responding if anything in the
      history is complicated.
  - problem: A supporting partner who has sponsored before
    consequence: >-
      There are limits on how often and how soon someone can support a partner for residence.
      Discovering one applies after lodging wastes a large fee.
    fix: Disclose it and check the position at the outset rather than hoping it will not surface.

faq:
  - q: Do we need to be married?
    a: >-
      No. Marriage, civil union and de facto relationships are all assessed on the same test:
      living together for 12 months in a relationship that is genuine and stable. A marriage
      certificate is evidence, not a substitute for the rest.
  - q: We have been together for years but only lived together for six months.
    a: >-
      Then you are six months short. The requirement counts living together, not the
      relationship's duration. A temporary partner visa may bridge the gap if your partner
      qualifies to support one.
  - q: How long does it take?
    a: >-
      INZ publishes 80% within seven months once lodged. Realistically six to nine months
      including requests for further information, on top of the 12-month qualifying period.
  - q: Can our children be included?
    a: >-
      Dependent children aged 24 or younger can be included, and those aged 21 to 24 must show
      financial dependence. Each needs their own medical and police certificates.
  - q: What if we get declined?
    a: >-
      Most partnership declines are evidentiary rather than a finding that the relationship is
      not real, which matters because it shapes what to do next. There are time limits on the
      options, so come early.
  - q: Is this faster than Skilled Migrant?
    a: >-
      Usually, and it does not depend on your occupation, qualifications or pay. If you qualify
      on both routes it is worth comparing, but for most couples this is the shorter path.
