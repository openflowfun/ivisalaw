intro: >-
  The fastest route to New Zealand residence, if your occupation is on it. Two tiers with very
  different outcomes: Tier 1 goes straight to residence, Tier 2 works toward it over two years.
  Which tier you are on is not a judgement about you — it is a line on a list that changes.

whoItsFor:
  lead: >-
    Occupation-led, not points-led. Either your role is on the list with requirements you meet,
    or it is not — there is no partial credit and nothing to make up elsewhere.
  fits:
    - Your occupation appears on Tier 1 or Tier 2 of the Green List.
    - You hold the specific qualification or registration that role requires.
    - You have a job or offer with an accredited employer at or above the required rate.
    - You are 55 or younger.
  doesntFit:
    - Your job title sounds similar to a listed role but the ANZSCO code differs.
    - You meet the experience but not the registration the role specifies.
    - You are self-employed, or the role is casual.
    - You are over 55 — no Green List route is available regardless of occupation.

eligibility:
  - title: Your occupation is on the Green List
    detail: >-
      The list is organised by ANZSCO occupation code, not job title. A role advertised as
      something close to a listed occupation is not the same thing. Check the code your duties
      actually map to before you build a plan on it.
    figure:
      value: Tier 1 or Tier 2
      label: Which tier decides everything
      note: Tier 1 goes straight to residence. Tier 2 requires 24 months of qualifying work first.
      source: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/requirements-for-work-visas/green-list-occupations-qualifications-and-skills/green-list-roles-jobs-we-need-people-for-in-new-zealand/
  - title: You meet that role's own requirements
    detail: >-
      Each listed occupation carries its own conditions — a named qualification, occupational
      registration, a minimum period of experience, or a pay rate specific to that role. These
      are per-occupation, not general. Meeting the spirit of them is not enough.
  - title: An accredited employer, at the right rate
    detail: >-
      The job must be with an employer holding Immigration New Zealand accreditation, and paid
      at the rate specified for the role — or the median wage where the role specifies none,
      whichever is higher. Employment must be permanent or fixed-term for at least 12 months,
      or contracts totalling at least six months.
    figure:
      value: $35.00 an hour
      label: Default floor where no rate is specified
      effective: 9 March 2026
  - title: Tier 2 only — 24 months of qualifying work
    detail: >-
      Two years working in New Zealand in the Tier 2 role, on an acceptable visa, meeting the
      role's requirements for the whole period. Any work visa counts, including an AEWV, as do
      Critical Purpose Visitor Visas with work rights and interim visas that follow a
      qualifying work visa. A gap where you fell below the required rate can reset the clock.
  - title: Age, English, health and character
    detail: >-
      You must be 55 or younger when you apply. English is required, as are a medical
      examination and chest x-ray for applicants aged 15 and over, and police certificates
      under six months old. Your partner and dependent children aged 24 or younger can be
      included in the application.

process:
  - actor: You
    title: Confirm the occupation and tier
    detail: >-
      Match your actual duties to an ANZSCO code, then check that code against the list. This
      step decides whether you are two months or two years from residence, so it is worth
      being certain rather than hopeful.
  - actor: You
    title: Check the role's specific requirements
    detail: >-
      Qualification, registration, experience and pay rate are set per occupation. If
      registration is required, start it early — registration bodies are slower than
      Immigration New Zealand.
  - actor: Your employer
    title: Accreditation and the job offer
    detail: >-
      The employer must hold accreditation, and the offer must meet the term and pay
      requirements. Check the published accredited employer list yourself.
    duration: See employer accreditation
  - actor: You
    title: Tier 2 — accrue the 24 months
    detail: >-
      Work the role on an acceptable visa, at or above the required rate, continuously. Keep
      payslips and employment records as you go; assembling two years of evidence afterwards
      is far harder than keeping it.
    duration: 24 months
  - actor: You
    title: Lodge the residence application
    detail: >-
      Tier 1 applicants can lodge as soon as the offer is in place. Tier 2 applicants lodge
      once the 24 months is complete and evidenced.
  - actor: Immigration NZ
    title: Assessment and decision
    detail: >-
      Assessed against the Green List requirements for your occupation as they stand when you
      apply — not when you started.
    duration: 80% within 3 months

whatGoesWrong:
  - problem: A job title that resembles a listed occupation
    consequence: >-
      The list works on ANZSCO codes and duties, not titles. People build two-year plans around
      a role that was never on the list, and find out at application.
    fix: >-
      Map your actual duties to a code first, then check the code. If it is borderline, get it
      assessed before you accept the job, not after.
  - problem: Meeting the experience but not the registration
    consequence: >-
      Many listed roles require occupational registration specifically. Years of experience do
      not substitute for it, and registration bodies work to their own timetables.
    fix: >-
      Start registration before the visa application, not alongside it. It is routinely the
      longest lead time in the process.
  - problem: Pay set at the median wage when the role specifies more
    consequence: >-
      Where a Green List role carries its own rate, that rate applies if it is higher. An offer
      at the general median wage fails the requirement for that occupation.
    fix: Check the role's specified rate before signing, and have the offer written to it.
  - problem: Tier 2 work that quietly stops qualifying
    consequence: >-
      A pay rate that falls below the requirement, a change of duties, or a gap between visas
      can mean months of the 24 do not count — usually discovered at the end.
    fix: >-
      Review the requirement against your actual pay and duties every time either changes, and
      keep payslips throughout rather than reconstructing them later.
  - problem: Assuming the list is fixed
    consequence: >-
      Occupations are added and removed. A Tier 2 plan started today is assessed against the
      list as it stands when you apply, not when you began.
    fix: >-
      Treat a two-year Green List plan as carrying policy risk, and have a Skilled Migrant
      assessment as a fallback rather than a surprise.
  - problem: Turning 56 mid-pathway
    consequence: >-
      The age limit is 55 or younger at application. Someone on a Tier 2 pathway who ages out
      before the 24 months completes has no Green List route left.
    fix: Work backwards from your 56th birthday before committing to a Tier 2 plan.

faq:
  - q: How do I know if my job is on the Green List?
    a: >-
      Check the ANZSCO code your duties map to, not your job title, against the published list.
      Each listed occupation also carries its own qualification, registration and pay
      requirements — being on the list is necessary but not sufficient.
  - q: What is the real difference between Tier 1 and Tier 2?
    a: >-
      Time. Tier 1 goes straight to residence on the strength of the job offer. Tier 2 requires
      24 months of qualifying work in New Zealand first. Same list, very different plans.
  - q: Does my employer have to be accredited?
    a: >-
      Yes. The job must be with an employer holding Immigration New Zealand accreditation, and
      you should verify that on the published list before you rely on it.
  - q: Can my family come with me?
    a: >-
      Your partner and dependent children aged 24 or younger can be included in the
      application. Each needs their own medical and police certificates, and those aged 16 and
      over have English requirements of their own.
  - q: What if my occupation is removed from the list?
    a: >-
      Applications are assessed against the list in force when you apply. That is the main risk
      in a Tier 2 plan, and the reason to keep a Skilled Migrant assessment alongside it.
  - q: Is the Green List always faster than Skilled Migrant?
    a: >-
      For Tier 1, almost always. For Tier 2, not necessarily — if you can reach six Skilled
      Migrant points sooner than 24 months, that route may finish first. Worth comparing rather
      than assuming.
