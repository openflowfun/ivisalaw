intro: >-
  Almost every work visa in New Zealand now runs through an accredited employer. Which one
  you need depends less on your occupation than on who is employing you and at what skill
  level the role sits.
lead: >-
  Start with the route that matches your situation. If none of them fits cleanly, that
  usually means the answer is a sequence rather than a single visa — which is what an
  assessment is for.
pathways:
  - title: Accredited Employer Work Visa
    slug: aewv
    note: The main route for skilled workers. Requires an accredited employer, a Job Check and a job token.
    tag: Most common
  - title: Post-Study Work Visa
    slug: post-study
    note: For graduates of eligible New Zealand qualifications. Open, not tied to one employer, and time-limited.
  - title: Partner of a Worker Work Visa
    href: /family/partner/
    note: Whether you can support a partner to work here turns on your role's skill level.
  - title: Employer accreditation
    href: /employers/accreditation/
    note: What your employer has to do before you can apply for anything.
chooser:
  lead: A rough orientation. None of this replaces an assessment of your actual circumstances.
  rows:
    - when: You have a job offer and the employer is accredited
      then: Accredited Employer Work Visa
    - when: You have a job offer but the employer is not accredited
      then: They need accreditation and a Job Check first
    - when: You have just finished a New Zealand qualification
      then: Post-Study Work Visa
    - when: Your partner already works in New Zealand
      then: Partner of a Worker Work Visa
    - when: You want to come without a job offer
      then: There is no general skilled work visa. The route is usually study, or a partner-based visa
